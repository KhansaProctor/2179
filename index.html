Beyond the Klang Valley

Public Transport in Peninsular Malaysia

Malaysia's public transport story is often focused on Kuala Lumpur and Klang Valley. This visualisation explores ridership patterns, network coverage, and service frequency across the peninsula, using data from data.gov.my and the Department of Statistics Malaysia.

Where do Malaysians use public transport?

Klang Valley dominates with over 100 trips per 1,000 residents per day, while most other states have negligible ridership.

When do KTM trains run?

Two clear peaks: a strong morning commute around 7am and a longer evening peak from 5–7pm.

Which services carry the most passengers?

The top six services are all in the Klang Valley. Outside the Klang Valley, only Rapid Bus Penang exceeds 20,000 daily trips.

Does ridership match population?

The Klang Valley's 9.6 million residents take more than 1.1 million public transport trips per day, while Johor with 4.2 million residents records only 8,300 daily trips.

States with no rail or Rapid Bus service are excluded from this view. Both axes use a logarithmic scale.

How has ridership grown since 2022?

Monthly average of daily public transport trips across all 13 services in Peninsular Malaysia. Ridership has a clear step up in early 2023 when the MRT Putrajaya Line opened.

How does ridership vary day to day?

Distribution of daily total public transport trips across Peninsular Malaysia from 2022 to 2026. The bimodal pattern reflects weekday vs weekend/public holiday ridership forming two groups of around 1.2–1.4 million trips and around 700k–900k.

Are there seasonal patterns?

Darker cells mean higher ridership. Outside of Klang Valley, ridership stays consistent. Within Klang Valley, July onwards seems to have higher ridership

How dominant is the Klang Valley?

The Klang Valley accounts for over 90% of all trips. Penang is the only other region with a visible share, while Johor, Pahang, and cross-state services together contribute less than 3%.

Bus or rail — what moves each region?

The Klang Valley's public transport is overwhelmingly rail-based, with rail services carrying more than four times the bus ridership. In contrast, Penang relies primarily on its Rapid Bus network.

How does ridership differ on weekdays vs weekends?

Commuter focused services like LRT and MRT see dramatic drops on weekends, while intercity services like KTM ETS maintain steadier demand.
